How St. George's Rome Kramer Got Recruited to UNLV

Category: College Recruiting

Tags: College Soccer Recruiting, UNLV, ODP, Soccer Academy, St. George Utah

Rome Kramer got recruited to play college soccer by doing three things: playing in showcase games, emailing coaches at almost every school in the country, and sending new highlight videos after every game. Today he is a freshman forward at UNLV. He started with the idea of playing in college after making an ODP regional team, and he spent time at Barca Academy in Arizona before UNLV.

Rome is from St. George, Utah, and trained with Coach Justin at STG Soccer Skills. We talked with him about college soccer recruiting, how to get recruited, and what he would tell parents whose kids want to follow the same path.

How do kids get recruited for college soccer?

Most players do not get found by accident. Rome played showcase games and reached out to college programs himself. He first started connecting with college coaches as a freshman in high school, and he kept at it for years.

"I got connected by just playing showcase games, emailing almost every school in the country, basically, to have them come watch me play." UNLV was one of the programs that came to watch. "UNLV came and watch me play. And brought me out on a visit."

The takeaway for parents is simple. Playing well is the starting point, and your player still has to get in front of coaches. Showcases give coaches a place to see them, and an email gives coaches a reason to show up.

When did Rome know he wanted to play college soccer?

For Rome, it clicked after ODP. He started working with Coach Justin, and within the first couple of months he was on an ODP regional team. Rome said that was the moment college became the goal.

"...after my first two months with Justin, he took me to ODP regional team. And after ODP regional team, I knew I wanted to go play college."

If your child is still deciding whether college soccer is for them, a higher level of competition like ODP can show them what is possible. It can show you too.

How do I make a soccer highlight video for college coaches?

Rome did not make one video and call it done. After every game he went back through the film, clipped new moments, and built a fresh highlight video. Then he sent it to coaches first thing the next morning.

"I would email every single night, making my highlight [reel]. Every game, I would go in and clip new videos of me. Send them out to coaches. I'll be up till one, two in the morning just making them, and then in the morning, I would send them out right away."

You do not need to stay up until two in the morning. But the habit is worth copying: keep your highlight video current, keep it short and clear, and send it while the game is still fresh. A coach who gets a new video after every game starts to remember the name.

Do showcases matter for college soccer recruiting?

They helped Rome. Showcase games gave college coaches a way to see him play, and his emails told them where to find him. Showcases work best when coaches already know your name and a specific game to watch.

Before you pay for a showcase, ask which college coaches will be there, then email those coaches ahead of time and tell them when your player is on the field.

Is a club academy better than high school soccer for getting recruited?

Rome went the academy route. He spent time at Barca Academy in Arizona, and he described it as very different from anything he had experienced. He said his teammates there included players who are now at schools like Wake Forest and UNC, and some who are pro.

Living on his own was part of it. He described a week that started with 6 AM training, then school until 3 PM, a team lift at 3:30, and training from 4 PM until 6 or 6:30. There was no school on Fridays, and games were on the weekend, depending on travel.

"That was way different than anything I've ever experienced. Best players in the country over there."

Asked what he would change, Rome's answer was clear. "Looking back, I would have left to go to an academy as soon as I could. As early as I could."

That is one player's opinion, not a rule for every family. An academy means living away from home and handling school and soccer on your own. Think hard about whether your child is ready for that, and talk to coaches you trust.

What if my child is an underdog?

Rome described himself that way. He said he always felt like the underdog, that he had to work his way into the team by getting a chance to play, and that once he got it, he held on.

"I've always been more of the underdog... once I got my chance, I never left the starting lineup. So I would say just have to grind it out. Always always grind it out."

Playing time is not handed out. Players earn it by being ready when the chance shows up. If your child is not getting minutes right now, that is not a reason to quit. It is a reason to keep training.

How do we find the right college soccer program?

Rome's advice was to look for a place where the school likes you, the coaches like you, and you like the environment. He said he loves it at UNLV.

"I love it here at UNLV. It's a great school. We have a great team and a great coach."

When you visit, ask the coaches where your player fits on the roster, and ask how they see him in the program. Pick the school as much as the team.

What is college soccer like compared to what he did before?

Rome said the biggest adjustment has been schoolwork. In his words, he could be a little relaxed about homework before, and now he cannot. On the field, he said college soccer is a little more physical.

Parents can help by having the schoolwork habits in place before college, not after.

What does Rome tell younger players to do right now?

He keeps it simple. Train every day, even if it is only juggling a ball. Listen to your coaches, because they are usually right. And work hard no matter what. "I would say that you need to work hard no matter what."
